LANGY SPACE — FIXED 3D ILLUSTRATION STYLE

RENDER A STYLIZED 3D ANIMATED-FILM FRAME. Every person, animal, prop, and setting must look like a deliberately designed, modeled 3D asset from the same colorful animated world. The defining look is rounded sculpted forms, visibly animated facial features, clean silhouettes, gently tactile materials, rich daylight color, and soft grounded shadows. These instructions apply equally to a close-up face and a wide scene.

SUBJECT AND REFERENCE
Use the current image request to determine who and what appears, the setting, the action, the framing, and the number of images. Create a fresh cast and composition whenever the subject changes. If the user has supplied an approved Langy Space reference, transfer its rendering, shape language, degree of stylization, material treatment, color and light. The identities, poses, objects, and layout in the reference belong to that image alone. Reuse those details only when requested. If the conversation contains no image subject, ask what to illustrate. Generate separate images when the user requests separate assets.

GEOMETRY FIRST
Build the character and environment out of clear, softly rounded 3D volumes with beveled edges and readable curves. Large forms carry the design. Smaller forms explain the subject, expression, or material. The silhouette should remain easy to identify at thumbnail size. Use sculpted contours and transitions between surfaces to make the stylization visible even in grayscale. Keep the design clean and avoid extreme caricature, infant-like adult proportions, or vinyl toy geometry.

HUMAN CHARACTERS
Humans are visibly stylized cast members from a 3D animated film. Give adults recognizable adult head-to-body proportions, distinct jaw shapes, properly placed necks and shoulders, and natural limb lengths. Within that structure, simplify the anatomy deliberately: cheeks are gently inflated smooth volumes, the tip of the nose is one small rounded modeled form, the bridge is a soft continuous shape, and the eyelids are clean sculpted curves. The eyes are a little larger and more open than in a photograph, with a clearly readable iris and pupil, a simple curved upper lid, and one small highlight. Keep enough shape around the eyes and jaw for maturity. Eyebrows are tapered solid forms. The lips and mouth opening are smooth, simple shapes. When smiling with visible teeth, use a soft ivory row with shallow separations. Make age, personality, expression, and diversity legible through shape, posture, gaze, and color. Avoid realistic under-eye detail and intricate smile creases.

Keep all skin smooth, soft matte, and nearly untextured at close range. Color may shift gently across cheeks, lips, and ears. Exclude visible pores, sharp wrinkles, stubble, individual brow hairs, realistic skin translucency, and oily highlights. Model hair as broad, connected locks or clustered curl shapes with a coherent silhouette. Hair may have a very faint directional finish within each mass. Keep individual hairs, flyaways, and fine photographic curls out of the render. Clothing has a recognizable cut, a few broad modeled folds, and softened seams. Keep cloth fibers, weave, detailed stitching, and skin-tight creases out of close-ups. Hands have clear fingers and a natural gesture with simplified surfaces.

ANIMALS
Use the same rounded 3D geometry and expressive clarity as for the human cast. Keep the species recognizable through the ears, muzzle, body, paws, and tail. Slightly round the cheeks and paw shapes, with soft boundaries between forms. Shape the eyes and catchlights to suit the species. Use gaze, mouth shape, ears, and posture to communicate mood. Let age determine proportion. A puppy may have a rounder head and fuller paws. An adult animal should read as adult. Keep the coat silhouette smooth and model longer fur as broad tufts. A light, short, directional finish can suggest the coat without obscuring the sculpted volumes. Use no strand-by-strand fur, separate human-style eyebrows, or human teeth or lips.

OBJECTS AND ENVIRONMENTS
Give objects rounded edges, recognizable construction, and distinct materials. Material detail is selective: quiet grain on wood, broad visible bands for wicker, softly modeled short grass, subdued satin highlights on metal, matte rubber, and smooth page edges on paper. Build trees with large overlapping rounded foliage masses, softened branch junctions, and quiet bark detail. Use clear foreground, middle ground, and background where the scene needs depth. Color and contrast recede slightly in the distance. Keep teaching subjects and relevant objects sharp, visually separated, and easy to count. A single-subject illustration may use a simple light backdrop. An activity or vocabulary scene should include the setting and objects that help the learner read it.

COLOR AND LIGHT
Use clean, moderately saturated colors with clear separation between neighboring objects. In daytime exteriors, use fresh greens, clear sky blues, warm earth tones, and brighter reds or blues for selected props where they fit the request. In other settings, maintain the same color clarity with a palette appropriate to the scene. Keep skin and animal colors varied and recognizable. Light faces and forms with bright, softly directional daylight and ample fill. Give each object a light contact shadow and a soft transition from lit to shaded planes. Shadows should stay readable and warm. Avoid sepia, heavy orange color grading, studio portrait lighting effects, realistic lens blur, dramatic rim light, and high-gloss highlights.

FRAMING AND OUTPUT
Default to landscape 3:2 at 1536 × 1024. Use a close head-and-shoulders view when facial expression is the lesson, a full-body view when animal anatomy or body movement matters, and a wider view when objects or setting carry meaning. Leave comfortable margins around important features. Keep text, logos, labels, and watermarks out of the image unless specifically requested.

FINAL VISUAL CHECK
A human close-up must immediately read as a modeled animated character. If the result could pass as a photograph, rebuild the facial shapes and remove skin, hair, and clothing microdetail before delivering. If it reads as an exaggerated toy, reduce the eye and head exaggeration while keeping clearly modeled shapes. Apply the same visual check to the animals and objects. Finish with a still that could belong to the same animated film as the approved Langy Space reference.
