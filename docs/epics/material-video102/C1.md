## C1.01 · When certainty sounds like a fact

**Na tela:** Watch Julian Treasure discuss speaking habits, then give balanced feedback

**Pratica:** Patterns, impressions and a constructive response

**Trecho:** [TED · Julian Treasure — How to speak so that people want to listen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eIho2S0ZahI) · 1:48–2:46

[Abrir local](http://127.0.0.1:8955/?page=s_video102#c1-01)

1. **What behaviour does the blamethrower metaphor describe?**  
   Dica: Notice where responsibility goes  
   Resposta: It describes passing responsibility for your actions to other people

2. **How does he distinguish dogmatism from a supported claim?**  
   Dica: Listen to the contrast between facts and opinions  
   Resposta: He describes dogmatism as presenting opinions as if they were facts

3. **Why might exaggeration make a message less credible?**  
   Dica: Connect the exaggeration example to trust  
   Resposta: Example: If everything is described as extraordinary, the listener may stop trusting the strength of the claim

4. **Give feedback on that habit without labelling the person**  
   Dica: Use tends to or comes across as, then invite a response  
   Resposta: Example: The message tends to sound certain before the evidence is clear. Could we make the level of confidence more explicit?
